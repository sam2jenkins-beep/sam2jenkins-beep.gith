Section No. One From the Author

From the Author

Good evening parental figures, and welcome to the Sam Jenkins Newsletter.

An overdue letter, in eight parts, from the desk of a son who has been quiet for too long, and would like, this once, to say more than fits in a text message.

By Samuel Jenkins · Live From Vieux-Port, Montreal

It’s been far too long since we’ve actually talked, and I’ve been quiet in a way that I know is either causing you anger/worry, or to think I don’t care enough to text. I want to say upfront that it has nothing to do with you, and although we’ve spoken about this countless times, I get the feeling you guys don’t actually believe me when I say that so I thought I would repeat it anyways.

I have enough self awareness to admit I am a horrible texter, but I’ve been particularly horrible lately, and had to sit down and really think about why this is the case, because I am completely paralyzed by it in a way I’ll talk about in a second. I know from this far away it’s hard to tell whether this means something or whether it’s just same old careless Sam.

Since its been a while, I’m trying something. I want to actually catch you up on my life, what’s happening at work, with my various meetings, with Kate, with my head, with the summer coming up, instead of another rushed Facetime where I bounce around to whatever my brain remembers in the moment and spending far too much time saying “I can’t remember if I told you this but…”

I’d love it if you sent me one back, but I know this is incredibly long so don’t feel pressure to match it because I cheated a bit haha. I wrote most of this by talking into my phone, I have an app that does the whole voice to text thing and I just pull it out when I have a moment to reflect on what you guys would care to know. The cool part of the app is it takes out all the filler umms and awws of my speech, so I can come out the other side with a decently structured paragraph and then just edit myself.

I want to know where you both are too, not just the headlines you sprinkle in between my rambling on Facetime haha.

I love and miss you both dearly, hopefully we can find time for that Montreal visit soon!

Here is Vol. 1 of my life update newsletter.

Section No. Two · A Manifesto on presence, hypocrisy, & the long quiet

The app I can’t stop thinking about.

The thing I’ve been wrestling with most lately is something I’d rather you hear from me while I’m in it than the after-the-fact revisionism of “how it was bad for a bit and then I was fine” which I find to be the case since I have been here.

As you know I’ve been working on this app idea and framework around being in the present moment in the background of my life. If you don’t quite remember what it was, the short version is:

I think a huge amount of what’s wrong with how people, especially younger people, are moving through the world right now is that they’re cognitively fried. Overstimulated, perpetually distracted, never quite where they are. I see it in my students every day. I see it on the metro, in restaurants, in my own home with Kate, and most glaringly I feel it in myself.

Trying to figure out what tool could actually help with that when a.) Every tool people sell nowadays is either scrolling content or an overstimulating iPhone app, and b.) the inability to break into the market of wellness without some sort of celebrity/expertise momentum to get started. There’s no point in building another meditation app when they are all pretty much the exact same but already have millions of subscriptions and in all honesty, fail to help you gain your attention back. Instead they reroute it to a 20 minute interlude in your day where Matthew McConaughey speaks sweet nothings in the form of guided mantras in your ears. Rather I want something built around the idea of getting the attention back.

That meeting I had a couple weeks ago with the kids dad was not only the first time I ever put myself out there in something other than teaching/coaching/basketball, but also the first time I’d said the idea out loud to anyone other than the residents of 117 Greenshire & my roommate, and it went well enough that I haven’t been able to stop thinking about it since.

I’m building the thing while I’m also the person who needs it most.

— the manifesto, more or less

Here is the part that’s been hard. It is very difficult to feel like the right person to build something about presence when I am, by any honest measure, not living it. I find myself picking up and putting down my phone constantly, stopping replying to someone in the middle of a back and forth convo and physically unable to walk George at night without bringing my Kindle.

I am the person I am describing and a little more thought helps me see just how deeply I am the cognitively fried adult version of the kids I’m worried about. The hypocrisy of it makes me feel like I need to change myself first in order to have the credibility to be a voice for this, but now I’ve gone quiet on the people I love. Its causing the same loop I want this app to address, the device pulling me away from the room I’m actually in, including the digital room where I’d be writing back to you.

I see the same thing in Kate, and watching it has clarified something for me. With the show airing, she has been in the absolute vortex of reading comments she knows she shouldn’t read, watching a spliced up version of herself get talked about in real time. It’s a self-destructive loop, and the hard part to watch is that the distraction of the scroll temporarily takes the edge off the discomfort it’s causing. Withdrawal from the phone is so real. I love her, and watching her go through this has shown me even more clearly than my own behaviour has, what this actually looks like from the outside.

So that’s where my head is. I’m building the thing while I’m also the person who needs it most, and am trying to be that person, but ending back in a different but equally unhealthy place. I don’t know yet whether I should be disqualified for this behaviour or if I actually needed the reality check of it for this idea to come about.

the manifesto needs me. and I, evidently, need the manifesto. ~ SJ

What I can tell you is that this is the bigger reason I’ve been so flaky for a while now. It isn’t that I don’t care enough to put the effort in, because god knows I put the mental effort in stressing about it Lol. It’s that I’ve failed to save my own attention thus far, but I am making progress now. I spend time thinking about how I use my phone, when I check Instagram, how I ought to be handling messages in a more healthy way.

I have turned to writing by hand a significant amount, which has been really great, and where the idea for this came from.

So there’s my manifesto that I wanted you to hear, and a Facetime definitely couldn’t have described it like this. If I just told you “sorry, I’ve been busy,” again that wouldn’t help anyone.

Section No. Three · The Classroom scratched into the chalkboard

Twenty days, and counting.

I am twenty days from the end of the school year, scratching each tick into the chalkboard with my absent finger nails.

Eight or nine classes with my grade sevens, somewhere between ten and twelve with the eights, and the back quarter of those are review. You would think since it is middle school we’d all be checked out by now, but no these kids have to write a full year exam in june, that is insane to me I don’t think I could pass the exam we are giving them right now. June fifth is my last day of teaching. June fourteenth is the last PED day, and then the year is officially closed.

Its been a pretty heavy few months since moving back here. I am certainly happy with my decision and have loved every aspect of the new Montreal adventure. Even if there are some similar characters to before like Daniel, Alex, Quarry, Jane etc. there is so much new all the time it has been such a long year of growth and exhaustion and learning from mistakes.

Going in to the year at Selwyn, I figured the classroom would be the mountain and everything else would settle around it. The actual experience has been the opposite. Teaching has been the easiest part of my job. Delivering the lessons, giving them something to enjoy in their dreadful school day, the standing-up-in-front-of-them part that I can do (when I’m not grouchy). What’s been hard is everything that surrounds it.

The grading pile that I’ve let mount up because I keep finding reasons not to start it is insane right now and thats how I’ve spent the last few days from 9pm-12am. That part is so different than Ridley, which was all digital, most of it was self marked by the CPU, I didn’t have to battle to read a 12 year old boys hand writing. The fact that I don’t have the same kind of staff-room friendships I had at Ridley, and I haven’t quite figured out how to cultivate anything close to as deep and meaningful as Andrew and Dereck and Victor is not surprising, but I thought I would be further along by now. I have my buddies from McGill who I have definitely had experiences with in the past so thats nice, but I don’t really see them. I started trying more recently now that I am pretty sure I am staying here next year. Unfortunately, the realization that I’ve been spending more of my school day in conversation than at my desk hammering out marking, and that I’ve been telling myself that counts as effort when really it’s been a long, slow procrastination dressed up as “trying”.

Andrew, Dereck, Victor. miss you guys.

The other piece that’s been wearing on me is that I haven’t loved coaching this year the way I usually do. Middle school, an all-boys school, and a particular kind of student demographic make for a specific cocktail of bad vibes most of the time. The kids are unserious in a way that’s probably developmentally normal, though I have no frame of reference, but exhausting to be the adult in front of.

They are not afraid of me unless I really blow the lid off the place, which I actually enjoyed at Ridley, because those kids were mature way beyond their years, but here it is a structural problem when you are trying to actually teach them something. They don’t take advice. They don’t believe anyone has ever felt what they’re feeling. They live, in the way that twelve and thirteen year old boys live in 2026, and you can either fight or build around it, and most days I’ve been doing a brutal impression of someone trying to build, but end up fighting the small stuff and it causes me way too much mental stress for no reason.

I’m presenting awards for my team at the Middle School Athletic Awards on Monday, June 1st. We are far enough removed now that I have nice things to say about the award winners, but when I picked them a few months ago it was harder to decide on them than if I was asked to describe the story of Dad picking bad grapes during the pandemic in arabic backwards.

I am not worried at all, and really don’t write this to have you worry in any way, honestly I haven’t really thought this deeply about it all until now, and its just been part of the daily mental grind. The job is good and hopefully going to get a whole lot better next year with some recent developments. I’m not in any kind of crisis about it YET, but you’ve heard me describe my work years before, and this one is for sure a new flavour to the Ridley experience. The cult life is great far enough removed hahaha.

I needed an easing-in year, and that’s what this has been mundane and stressful in roughly equal measure, but the balance of that scale has just been all over the place.

Section No. Four · The Academy six months of silence, then a single yes

The academy is real now.

I spent most of last fall and the first part of winter pushing on this thing, like really pushing, and getting almost nothing back. No clear yes, no clear no, no real guidelines, no decisions. Just months of me drafting and proposing and following up into a void.

I had basically resigned myself to the idea that it wasn’t going to happen this year and started telling myself the work would be useful for some future version of the project, somewhere else, sometime later.

Then a few weeks ago, in a single meeting, Mike basically said, “Okay, let’s do it.”

If I want it shaped, I have to shape it.

— the thing the year taught me

Next Saturday, I’m running a double tryout for the academy. We’ve invited every grade seven boy who’s coming back next year and every grade six boy who’s either coming up from Selwyn or new to the school and wants to play. I’m running a half tryout in the morning, a pizza lunch in the middle to keep the day humane, and then I’m cutting from there.

The roster can be any size three kids, eight, twelve. The goal of the day is just to find out who actually wants this, and then I get to choose. At least that’s what I think…

The piece that’s been frustrating is that the school has not yet defined what “this” is. I’m asking eleven and twelve year old boys to commit to something, and through them I’m asking their parents to commit to it too, and I genuinely cannot tell either of them what they are committing to.

Is this a second-term sport team next year? A spring commitment? A year-round thing with morning practices and a real schedule? I don’t know yet, and I can’t run a tryout in good conscience without being able to answer that question.

So tomorrow I’m dropping in on Mike to push for clarity. I’d rather have an awkward conversation now than walk into Saturday holding a clipboard and a vague answer. The thing I’ve learned this year is that nobody is going to define this for me. If I want it shaped, I have to shape it.

The bigger picture, the part that actually has me excited, is what this opens up for next year. If the academy lands, there’s a real path to me holding two contracts at the school, my teaching one and a separate one for the academy work. That would mean significantly more on my plate, and significantly more income, and a much clearer answer to the question of what I’m actually doing with my life professionally.

I’m not there yet. But I can see it, and that visibility alone has changed how I feel about the year I just had. The mundane and stressful months start to look less like a lost year and more like the runway I needed.

Section No. Five · Kate she gets to be herself again on Monday

The last week of the show.

Kate gets voted out on this week’s episode, and we already know because the network gave her the full week of episodes on Monday until the wee hours of the morning. We watched together at home, which was a strange experience, sitting on the couch next to her while a version of her on screen lives through a moment she can’t change anymore.

Tomorrow she’s heading to the Bell office to do her post-game interviews. The format is an hour-long post-show breakdown and a podcast that runs right after the episode airs Sunday night. She and the other contestant who gets voted out tomorrow each go on back-to-back with a Quebecois celebrity host, no idea who that is, then the two of them come on together at the end. So Sunday night is her last episode, surely to be stressful.

Being on the show has ended up being much harder than I think either of us anticipated. The version of her edit that has been aired put her in the orbit of the season’s villains not as someone whose character is being attacked, but as someone whose alignment puts her in the splash zone of her alliance’s reputation.

She doesn’t get to defend herself until this post show episode airs tonight, and the people commenting online are not interested in the intricacies of her strategy unfortunately. Honestly she has been a shell of her usual self confidence-wise for weeks now, and watching that without anything to do to help, and often receiving some of the frustration has been the tough part for myself.

The pressure to post online and to participate publicly in her own narrative, to engage with the “fans”, to do the thing that other contestants are doing and is essentially expected of her has been enormous, and she has mostly not been able to do it. Wanting to post and not posting is a paralysis that has bled into other parts of her life. She has been quieter, more inward, not going out with friends as much, less herself.

And she gets to stop watching herself every day. I get to stop watching her every day. We both get to stop refreshing things.

— the part I’m most looking forward to

What I am most looking forward to is the part that comes after Sunday. Her visibly active part in the show wraps. The conversation about her lasts another week, maybe ten days, and then it moves on. And she gets to stop watching herself every day. I get to stop watching her every day. We both get to stop refreshing things.

Section No. Six · At Home workouts, sleep, & the housing question

Life inside the little box.

Outside of work and Kate’s ever-developing relationship with Survivor, life has been extremely full which is great. I feel like as I’m writing this it reads like my life is a disaster but it is not, it’s really fun and exciting.

I’ve been working out at least four times a week, and my body and head are both better for it. My men’s league team finished the season by winning the championship, which I am extremely proud of, though haven’t picked up a basketball competitively since.

That’s been the one weird side effect of the weather warming up, usually spring is when I’d be finishing up and then getting into off-season grind, and instead I’ve stopped almost entirely. I’ve decided not to do any summer leagues this year because the math doesn’t work. It is between two-fifty and three hundred dollars for a season, and a realistic time frame of mid-June to mid-August when I’m actually here, I’d be paying full price for half a season and getting none of the consistent social and exercise benefits out of it.

I’d rather save the money and channel the basketball energy and time into other things like the academy work, which is going to need it.

Sleep has been the one piece of my health that’s a little touch and go. Not in a worrying way. Just a steady stream of nights where my brain is busy with ideas and refuses to power down on schedule. It’s a cost I’ve been dealing with since probably November, of having more things I actually care about than I can finish in a day.

The other thread of life at home is one that’s been quietly building for a few weeks and is going to need real conversation soon. We just spent close to three weeks at Kate’s parents’ place while they were away, and the experience made it embarrassingly clear how cramped our current apartment is. We’ve been calling it the little box for a while as a joke, but after three weeks of actually having space, the joke is landing a little differently.

Alex’s voice in my head: do not, under any circumstances, buy a condo without running the numbers twice.

So the housing question is officially on the table. Not urgently, but seriously. Buy something for ourselves which is a whole new can of worms, or keep our current place as a rental and move into a rented space bigger than here. I only offer option two because of how much Alex has brainwashed me about the cost of owning, it’s something I have not looked into on my own yet so it’s not an opinion I hold, just what the real estate expert in my life says.

There’s obviously a very different financial shape and timeline to these, and Kate and I have not yet sat down to have the real version of that conversation. We’ve been circling it but with everything else and the weddings this summer, now is not the time so we will make it work here. Part of what I’m hoping for from this summer is the headspace and time to actually have it.

Section No. Seven · The Summer Ahead Ottawa, Nice, & the rest of the picture

The shape of the summer.

June 5th is my last day of teaching, and I am counting them down hour by hour. June 14th is the last PED day, but it seems to be very light on this end of the year and once that’s done I’m officially done until the end of August.

That gives me a real ten-ish week summer, which I haven’t actually had in a long time. All of my teaching summers so far have had something ongoing, coaching commitments, certifications, moves, transitions. This one I have a little more say over.

Right after the year ends, the summer opens with the big wedding trip. I am flying to Nice on June 24th, Kate is joining on the 27th I believe, and staying through July 14th. Jane and Daniel are getting married on the 4th or 5th, and their wedding this weekend in Ottawa is the local one. It was a hilarious and amazing evening, I spoke to you about it on the phone today so I’ll spare the repeated explanation, but I put some extra pics in here haha.

first time in France for me. wide eyes confirmed.

We don’t have the whole France itinerary nailed down yet. We’ve been talking about a few different shapes, for sure we are staying close to Nice and doing day trips, and real do-nothing days.

What I know for sure is that I want to come back from this trip having actually rested a bit, at least towards the end of it. Being a Survivor therapist has earned me that right!

It’s the most excited I’ve been about a stretch of months in a long time.

— the whole point, really

When we get back in mid-July, the rest of the summer will likely be the academy. Real planning, real structure, real time to think about what next year’s two-contract version of my life actually looks like. There will probably be some sort of training camp, there will be the housing and financial conversations between Kate and I and then, before I know it, August will be through and I’ll be back at Selwyn beginning year 2 with, I hope, a much clearer idea of what I want it to be.

That’s the picture. It’s the most excited I’ve been about a stretch of months in a long time.
